The Practice Group and Theory Reading Group accompany and extend the research of the Reasoning and Decision-Making Lab at MBZUAI, led by Professor Junpei Komiyama.

Through expository presentations and open discussion, we study papers, methods, and ideas connected to the lab’s research and to the broader interests of its members.

01 Purpose

Why we meet

A shared space around the lab’s research.

The lab develops principled machine-learning methods for reliable reasoning and decision-making under uncertainty. The reading groups provide a recurring forum in which we can explain research carefully, examine its assumptions, connect results across areas, and explore directions beyond our current projects.

The scope is intentionally broad. A meeting can center on any subject that meaningfully connects to the lab’s research or is of interest to its members.

Possible directions—not a fixed syllabus

  • LLM reasoning and post-training
  • Reinforcement learning
  • Multi-armed bandits
  • Information theory
  • Sequential decision-making
  • Stochastic search
  • Mechanism design
  • Algorithmic bias
  • Machine-learning methodology
02 The groups

Complementary perspectives

Two groups, one research conversation.

A topic may have both theoretical and practical aspects. Participants are welcome to attend either or both groups.

Practice Group01

Methods, systems, and empirical questions.

The Practice Group considers the empirical, methodological, and systems aspects of research. Sessions may examine how a method works in practice, how it is implemented and evaluated, what its limitations are, and what can be learned from reproducing or extending it.

Theory Reading Group02

Concepts, models, and proofs.

The Theory Reading Group concentrates on mathematical and conceptual foundations. Sessions work through definitions, models, assumptions, proofs, and the relationships between theoretical results.

04 Participation

Open conversation

Present, discuss, or simply listen.

The meetings are open to anyone interested in the topics or in learning more about the Reasoning and Decision-Making Lab. You are welcome to propose and present a paper or subject that you would like to explore, participate in the discussion, or simply attend, listen, and ask questions.

You do not need to be a current lab member or commit to presenting in order to attend. Students and researchers who are considering joining the lab are especially welcome.
